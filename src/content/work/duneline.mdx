<Brief>
Luxury rentals sell a feeling, and a grid of cars doesn't convey one. Duneline had to make browsing feel like the night drive itself, then get out of the way for a booking form with clear, live prices.
</Brief>

<Features>
  <Feature title="Scrolling is the drive" image="/work/duneline/feature-1.jpg" alt="The car on a night road, seen from above, beside a card of performance figures">
    The car starts parked in the hero and drives down a night road as you scroll, stopping beside four cards.
  </Feature>
  <Feature title="Three cars" image="/work/duneline/feature-2.jpg" alt="Three fleet cards with car silhouettes and daily prices">
    The fleet as three cars with daily prices, each leading straight into the booking form.
  </Feature>
  <Feature title="Live pricing" image="/work/duneline/feature-3.jpg" alt="The booking form with dates, delivery location, add-ons and a running total">
    Dates, delivery location and add-ons change the total as they are chosen, with ten percent off a week or more.
  </Feature>
</Features>

<Decisions>
  <Decision n="01" title="One timeline, driven by scroll" tradeoff="It relies on smooth scrolling (Lenis) to keep the car steady on trackpads.">
    The scroll position sets the car's place on the road through one GSAP timeline, so the drive stays in step whether you scroll fast, slowly or backwards.
  </Decision>
  <Decision n="02" title="One car, seen from above" tradeoff="Less spectacle on the first screen.">
    An earlier version had a side view in the hero and a top view for the drive, and two cars read as two ideas. The car now starts parked in the hero and drives from there.
  </Decision>
  <Decision n="03" title="A front end, said plainly" tradeoff="It can't take a real booking: return dates have no upper limit and the reference is a placeholder.">
    Prices, the seven-day discount and the request are all worked out in the browser, and nothing is saved. The case says so rather than implying a booking system.
  </Decision>
</Decisions>
